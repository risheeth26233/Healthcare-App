Prenatal Wellness

  • Nutrition: Focus on folate-rich foods, iron, calcium, and protein. Prenatal vitamins as recommended by your provider.
  • Hydration: Aim for 8-10 glasses of water daily. Proper hydration supports amniotic fluid and blood volume.
  • Movement: Gentle activities like walking, prenatal yoga, and swimming can improve circulation and mood.
  • Rest: Listen to your body. 7-9 hours of sleep plus naps when needed supports healthy development.
💝

Your Wellbeing Matters

  • Mental Health: Pregnancy brings many emotions. Talk to your provider about anxiety, mood changes, or depression.
  • Stress Management: Breathing exercises, meditation, and connecting with support systems can help.
  • Body Changes: Every pregnancy is unique. Trust your body's wisdom and communicate concerns with your care team.
  • Partner/Support: Involve your partner or support person in appointments and preparation.
📋

Preparing for Your Appointment

  • Write down questions between visits so you don't forget.
  • Track symptoms: nausea, swelling, fetal movements, contractions.
  • Bring a list of medications, supplements, and any test results.
  • Note your last menstrual period and any previous pregnancy history.
  • Consider bringing a support person for advocacy and note-taking.
❓

Questions You May Want to Ask Your Doctor

  • What prenatal tests or screenings are recommended for me?
  • What are the warning signs I should call about immediately?
  • What is your policy on inductions, C-sections, and birth preferences?
  • What pain management options are available during labor?
  • Who covers for you when you're not available?
  • What postpartum support and follow-up do you provide?
🌬️

Take a Moment to Breathe

Try this simple grounding technique:

1 Inhale slowly through your nose for 4 counts
2 Hold gently for 4 counts
3 Exhale slowly through your mouth for 6 counts
4 Repeat 4-6 times
🚨

When to Seek Immediate Care

Contact emergency services or go to the nearest ER if you experience:

  • Heavy vaginal bleeding
  • Severe abdominal pain or cramping
  • Sudden, severe swelling in face, hands, or feet
  • Severe headache that won't go away
  • Vision changes (blurring, flashing lights)
  • Fever over 100.4°F (38°C)
  • Decreased or no fetal movement
  • Signs of preterm labor before 37 weeks

When in doubt, always call your provider or seek emergency care. It's better to be checked and reassured than to wait.
